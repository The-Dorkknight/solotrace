# Breakout boards for the ESP32 "postage stamp" modules (0.9.0)

What was asked: a breakout for the ESP32-S3 and ESP32-C3 stamp-sized boards, with a few reachable + and - rails, power in through a 2-pin screw terminal *or* a 2-pin header, and every GPIO with **four** possible connections, "a hybrid of deadbug and through hole".

Everything below is from the repository's own data (the module notes in [mcu-modules.md](mcu-modules.md) and the parts' code), checked by tests. Nothing here has been built on a real board.

## Which modules

| Module (part id) | Pins | Power pins | Source of the pin order |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 and ESP32-C3 (`MOD_XIAO_S3`, `MOD_XIAO_C3`) | 2 x 7, rows 15.24 mm apart | right column, top: 5V, GND, 3V3 | Seeed's KiCad footprint; both chips share it (mcu-modules.md) |
| ESP32-C3 SuperMini (`MOD_ESP32C3_SUPERMINI`) | 2 x 8 | right column, top: 5V, GND, 3V3 | two independent footprints; one maker's file disagrees (see the part note) |
| Waveshare ESP32-S3-Zero (`MOD_ESP32S3_ZERO`) | 2 x 9 | **left** column, top: 5V, GND, 3V3 | two community footprints; Waveshare publishes the pinout only as a picture |

The XIAO ESP32-S3 and ESP32-C3 have the same footprint, so one breakout (built on `MOD_XIAO_S3`) serves both; the board and its pin labels are the same. The two other modules are added so the same generator covers the stamp-size boards people actually buy.

## The idea: a hole strip

A normal breakout is a row of single holes, one per pin. Here each module pin gets a **hole strip**: one bar of copper (1.8 mm wide, rounded ends) with holes every 2.54 mm. Because it is one piece of copper, every hole of a strip is the same net, so a pin has:

1. the module's own pad (the module sits in the middle, through-hole or flat on the copper),
2. and **four holes** on its strip (`BRK_STRIP4_LEAD`).

Each hole is a through-hole (pin header, jumper wire, component lead) *and* flat copper (a wire or SMD part soldered flat on the bar, deadbug style), which is the "hybrid of deadbug and through hole". That makes 4 places per GPIO besides the module's own pad. The bar starts 0.3 mm past the module's pad, so the pad and the bar overlap and are one piece of copper: no trace is needed between them (SoloTrace joins touching copper of the same net).

Strips are not in the BOM (they are copper and holes, not parts), always drilled and always flat copper: the *Pads of this part* choice does not apply to them.

Also added: plain strips of 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 and 16 holes (`BRK_STRIP<n>`) under *Board bits > Hole strips (breakout)*, for rails and your own breakouts.

## The rails and power in

- Three vertical rails of 6 holes: **3V3, GND and 5V**, on the side of the module where its power pins are. They are stepped (the 3V3 rail is nearest and shortest to reach, 5V the farthest) so each power row reaches its rail with one short straight trace and no rail is in another's way.
- **Power in:** a 2-pin screw terminal (`TERM_5.08_2`, 5.08 mm pitch) and a 2-pin header (`HDR_1x2`) are both wired to 5V and GND. Fit one of them. Pin 1 is 5V and pin 2 is GND on both (silk/pin labels show it).
- 3V3 has a rail but no connector: it comes from the module.

**Not verified here:** what happens if you power the module from the screw terminal and USB at the same time. Seeed's symbol calls the XIAO's 5V pad VBUS (mcu-modules.md), which suggests it is the USB 5 V line, but whether it can be back-fed safely was not checked in a datasheet. Read your module's documentation before feeding 5V to it while USB is plugged in. 3V3 is the module's own regulator output; its current limit was not looked up either.

## No copper under the module

Many of these modules carry pads or a battery connector on the *back* (the part notes say the pads under the board are not included in the footprint). So no copper and no trace goes under the module body: the strips stand clear to the left and right of it, and the power connectors sit below it. A test checks that no trace point lies inside the module's body rectangle.

## Layout sizes

Generated by `demos.breakout()` from the module's own pad table; sizes at the time of writing (mm): XIAO 52 x 30, SuperMini 52 x 31.5, S3-Zero 52 x 32.5. All three routed with no jumpers and no DRC problems; see `tests/test_breakout.py`.

## What was tried first

Four full-height rails routed badly (5V failed, traces wandered along the top, then through the middle under the module). Three short stepped rails fixed that, and moving the connectors under the module fixed the rest.

## Limits and open points

- Never etched, never soldered, no module plugged in. The strip hole spacing (2.54 mm) and 1.0 mm drills follow the rest of the library.
- The three breakouts are generated with the module's own pads *and* hole strips, so a module soldered flat (deadbug) lands on the strips' copper where they overlap its pads; a module on header pins stands in the module's own holes.
- A footprint saved from the custom-footprint text editor does not keep a pad's extra holes (the `holes` list is read and written only in files and the built-in parts).
- The 3V3 and 5V rails are plain copper bars: their current rating was not worked out. Power class traces use the usual 2 mm width.
