## 0.10.0-wip (2026-11-10)

- **PCB trace antennas with copper keep-outs.** A new group *Board bits > Antennas and keep-outs* (15 parts): a **meander monopole** for 315, 433.92, 868, 915 and 2450 MHz (one 1 mm trace, quarter-wave length from `c / (4 f sqrt(eps_eff))` with `eps_eff = 2.7` for FR4, plus 10 % to trim, silk ticks every 2 mm from the tip), a **wire whip** (a straight quarter-wave wire, in the parts list) for the same five bands, and five bare **keep-out boxes** (10x10 to 40x20 mm) for the antenna end of a module. The formulas are rules of thumb: nothing was etched or measured, and the parts say so. See [docs/research/pcb-antennas.md](docs/research/pcb-antennas.md).
- **Keep-outs are real.** A part can carry copper strokes (`strokes`), keep-out boxes (`keepouts`) and pins that are joined by a wire (`ties`), all saved in `.solotrace` files and custom parts. Copper of any kind inside a keep-out is a DRC error (kind `keepout`, also shown while moving a part), the ground fill and the back-side ground plane are cut away from it, the autorouter treats it as a hard obstacle and auto-tidy keeps traces out. It is drawn as an orange hatched box with a label in the desktop window and the web UI.
- **A parametric PCB coil generator** (*Parts > PCB coil generator...* in the desktop app, *Parts > PCB coil generator...* in the web menu). Give the outer size and EITHER the inductance wanted (it finds the turns and the track pitch) OR the number of turns (it works out the inductance), plus the track width, smallest gap, copper thickness and an optional frequency (it prints the parallel capacitor that resonates the coil). It saves the coil as a part under *My parts* and arms it for placement. The inductance is a straight-bar method (Rosa self inductance, Grover mutual inductance), cross-checked against the square-spiral formula of Mohan et al. (1999): within 3 % on three test coils. It is an estimate for a single-layer coil in air, **not measured**. On a one-sided board the inner end is carried out by an insulated wire between pins 2 and 3 of the part; the part counts those pins as joined.
- Two new examples (*File > Open example*, also in `examples/`): a 433.92 MHz meander antenna test board with ground fill, and a 30 x 20 mm 1.5 uH coil with a 13.56 MHz tuning capacitor footprint.
- Fix: the library-size test now leaves out the antennas and the breakout hole strips. 22 new tests (antenna parts and formula, keep-outs in DRC / fill / plane / router, save and load, web dialog, coil engine and both design modes).
- Not done: inverted-F, chip, patch and loop antennas, matching-network parts, NFC reader matching, round or multi-layer coils, and keep-out sizes for particular Wi-Fi modules (read them from the module's datasheet and use a keep-out box). The custom-footprint text editor drops strokes, keep-outs and ties of a part it re-saves.

## 0.9.0-wip (2026-11-07)

- **A green lock circle when wiring.** With the Wire tool the pin your click would land on is now marked before you click: a solid green circle with the pin name (and its net, in the web UI) appears on it, the rubber band snaps to it, and a grey dashed ring marks the pin you started from. The lock reaches about 14 screen pixels past the pad edge (clamped to 1.4 to 3 mm on the board, so it stays easy to hit when zoomed out and does not grab the wrong pin when zoomed in) and takes the nearest pad by distance to its edge, not to its centre. - **Hole strips and breakout boards.** New part kind `strip`: one bar of copper with several holes, one pin. A pad may now list its holes (`PadDef.holes`, saved in `.solotrace` files and custom parts), and `Board.drills()` (Gerber, laser, preview, web) includes all of them. Library: `BRK_STRIP2` to `BRK_STRIP16` (2 to 16 holes) and `BRK_STRIP4_LEAD` (4 holes, starting 0.3 mm past a module pad), under *Board bits > Hole strips (breakout)*. Strips are always through-hole and flat copper, never in the BOM, skipped by auto-place and by the ring check.
- **Three generated breakout boards** (*File > Open example*): XIAO ESP32-S3 / C3, ESP32-C3 SuperMini and ESP32-S3-Zero. Every module pin gets a 4-hole strip touching its pad (four extra connections per GPIO, through-hole and deadbug in one), three 6-hole rails (3V3, GND, 5V), and a 2-pin screw terminal and a 2-pin header for 5V / GND in. No copper under the module. They route with no jumpers and no DRC problems, and are exported to `examples/breakout-*` (Gerber, SVG, PDF, BOM, laser). Never etched or built; the XIAO 5V / USB question is left open on purpose (see [docs/research/breakout-boards.md](docs/research/breakout-boards.md)). Tests: `tests/test_breakout.py`.
- **PDF guide with every release.** `tools/make_pdf.py` builds `docs/SoloTrace-guide.pdf` from the README, changelog and notes, and the release workflow attaches it.

## 0.8.0-wip (2026-11-06)

- **A web app: nothing to install.** The whole editor now also runs inside a web page on the project's GitHub Pages site (`https://the-dorkknight.github.io/solotrace/app/`): Pyodide 0.29.5 (Python in WebAssembly, from the jsDelivr CDN) runs the same SoloTrace code in a Web Worker, so the page stays responsive. Nothing is uploaded: *Save* downloads, *Open* reads a file you pick, settings, favourites, your parts and the autosave stay in the browser's storage. Slower than the download and auto-route has no Cancel; tested in Chromium with a local copy of Pyodide, **not yet loaded from the CDN** (the build machine cannot reach it). Built by `tools/build_web_app.py` into `docs/app/`; `tests/test_web_app.py` checks it is up to date, that its Python runs without tkinter, threads or sockets, and (with Playwright and a local Pyodide) loads, routes and saves a board in Chromium.
- **The browser is the default.** `python3 src/main.py` and the three launchers always open the web UI now, so tkinter is no longer needed; `python3 src/main.py desktop` opens the old window (and falls back to the browser if tkinter is missing). The launchers only check for Python 3.8+.
- **Collisions after moving a part over laid traces.** Traces do not follow a part, and moving, turning, nudging, mirroring, duplicating or placing a part over existing copper said nothing until the next *Check*. Now, in both UIs, the pads and traces it collides with are ringed in red while you drag (the web UI shows a red ghost), and the status bar says how many collisions there are when you let go, with what to do (drag it clear, or *Route this part's nets*); auto-place reports them too. *Check* itself was measured and never missed one (64 random moves, compared with a brute-force check of every pair of copper shapes). New in *Check*: a module mounted under the board (mirrored) and soldered flat is pressed on the copper, so its body over another net's trace is a warning (a note on header pins).
- Fix (web UI): pressing on a trace that has branches joined to it got no answer from the server (its drag description held Python objects that cannot be sent as JSON), so such a trace could not be selected or dragged in the browser. Found while taking the 0.8.0 screenshots; test added.
- **Auto-route: measured against the first versions, made repeatable, and a polish pass.** The question was whether auto-route had got lazier since the first releases. Measured on 12 boards (the four oldest examples, each as built and with two shuffled placements), routed by 0.3.4 (the oldest version in the repository), 0.4.0 to 0.7.0 and this one: 0.3.4 used 64 jumpers in all and left 3 connections unrouted; 0.7.0 used 44 and left none, with the same total copper (4 515 mm against 4 509 mm). On the small boards it sometimes takes a longer path to save a jumper (it always prefers fewer jumpers first). What was wrong: **the same board did not route the same way twice.** The order in which ripped-up nets were rerouted came from a Python set, whose order changes every time Python starts, so one board could come out with 2 jumpers on one run and 4 on the next (measured). Fixed: the order is now sorted, so a board always routes the same way. New: after the best try is picked, a **polish pass** reroutes each net once against all the others and keeps the new copper only if it has fewer jumpers or is at least 1 mm shorter (it never makes a board worse, and Cancel during it puts back what it was trying): on the same 12 boards 42 jumpers and 4 498 mm of copper, up to 4 % less copper on the logic boards. `tests/test_router_quality.py` checks the repeatability across hash seeds, that polish never adds jumpers or copper, and that cancelling it loses nothing.

## 0.5.0-wip (2026-11-05)

Four things the author asked for, each researched first (the notes are in `docs/research/`):

- **Pro button: SMD parts.** A *Pro* toggle beside the parts search adds an *SMD (Pro)* group of 41 common, hand-solderable surface-mount footprints: chips 0603 to 2512, SMA/SMB/SMC, SOD-123/123F, MELF and MiniMELF diodes, SOT-23 (3, 5, 6 pins), SOT-223, DPAK (TO-252), D2PAK (TO-263), SOIC-8/14/16 narrow, SOIC-16 to 28 wide, SOP-8 5.3 mm, tantalum A to D, SMD electrolytics 4 to 10 mm, two shielded inductors. Every pad size comes from a maker's land-pattern drawing (TI, Diodes Inc, Vishay, KEMET, Nichicon, Sumida; the roomier wave-solder / level-A patterns where there was a choice) and every pad-to-pad gap is at least 0.3 mm, so nothing needs a solder mask. Packages finer than that (SOT-363, TSSOP, TQFP) are left out on purpose. SMD parts sit on the **copper side**: they are mirrored in the component-side view (press M to see them as you solder), their outlines go to a bottom silkscreen Gerber, and they have no holes. SoloTrace stays a through-hole program first: Pro is off by default and only adds parts. Pin-label presets for SOT-23, SOT-223, DPAK and the SOICs. New example *SMD regulator (Pro parts)*.
- **Thermal reliefs on ground pads** (ground fill and back plane): a ring of bare board round each ground pad bridged by spokes, so the iron does not have to heat the whole board. Defaults follow KiCad (four spokes, 0.5 mm ring) but the spokes are 0.8 mm because ground carries the return current: four of them are good for about 8 A at a 10 C rise (IPC-2221). *Ground fill settings* has the spoke count, width and ring, an **"or size spokes for N amps"** box that works the width out from the IPC-2221 formula, and shows the current the relief carries. Round pads get radial spokes at 45 degrees, ovals and rectangles (deadbug pads, copper islands) get theirs off the flat sides, big islands get spokes scaled to their size. Each part can be set to **heavy** (spokes twice as wide) or **solid** (no relief: high current, RF decoupling) in its panel. Check warns when the ground net's trace class is good for more current than its reliefs carry. A spoke that would touch another net is left out; a pad that would lose all its spokes stays solid.
- **Back-side ground plane for a double-clad board** (*Board > Back-side ground plane*). All traces stay on the copper side; the component side is left as one sheet of ground copper. Ground leads that pass through the board are joined by the plane and soldered on both sides; a new **Via tool (V)** adds vias (a hole with a bit of wire soldered both sides) for deadbug / SMD ground pins and for stitching; every other hole gets a bare ring in the plane (default hole + 1.1 mm: 0.25 mm electrical plus a 0.3 mm registration allowance). Buttons add a via at every ground pin without a hole and a stitching grid; the dialog says up to what frequency a spacing keeps the lambda/20 rule and explains the "big LC circuit" worry in two sentences (two copper sheets joined at one point resonate near 700 MHz on a 100 mm board; joined at every ground pin plus a grid they are a low-impedance mesh). Exports: a top-copper Gerber (plane with clearance holes and reliefs), 1:1 back-plane PDF/SVG, a back-plane laser job (not mirrored), and a **countersink list** for the registration-free way: leave the back solid, drill, and clear the copper round each non-ground hole with a 2 to 2.5 mm drill tip. Check reports vias cut by a clearance ring, ground leads trapped in one, a plane nothing joins, and how far the stitching is good for. New example *RF oscillator (back plane)*.
- **Auto-route algorithm (Board menu):** *Auto* (default), *Negotiated congestion* and *Classic*. Negotiated congestion is the PathFinder method used by FPGA tools: every net is routed at once, overlaps allowed, then the price of every contested cell rises pass after pass until the nets have sorted themselves out; jumpers join in as a priced resource once contested copper is dear enough. It does not depend on the net order, so it needs no retries. On eleven test boards it routed everything (classic left 2 connections), used the same number of jumpers (55), 7 to 9 % less copper, and took about two-thirds of the time; on the logic demo 5 jumpers instead of 7. *Auto* runs it first and falls back to the classic tries only if something is left or a jumper was needed, so it is never worse than before. Details and numbers in DESIGN_NOTES.md.
- **Auto-place algorithm (Board menu):** *Annealing* (default, unchanged) and *Routability* (experimental): the same moves plus a RUDY-style estimate of where copper will not fit and a penalty for pads boxed in by other parts, with VPR's adaptive cooling. **Measured no better than Annealing** on the test boards (sometimes worse), so it is off by default; it is there to try and to improve.
- The DRC window now also shows notes (severity "info"), such as the stitching-spacing guide; they do not count as problems.
- Command line: `route --algorithm auto|classic|negotiated`, `place --placer anneal|routability`.

## 0.4.1-wip (2026-11-02)

Bug and edge-case sweep of 0.4.0: a randomised test of about 400 boards (random parts, islands and ground-fill settings, routed and exported in every mode) and about 4 000 random editor actions (clicks, drags, Shift + clicks, right-click menu items, keys, undo/redo, view flips, ground fill on/off), plus targeted checks. Fixed:

- **Ground fill could take seconds per edit on a big board** (about 1 s on a 160 x 100 mm, 30-part test board, more on a slow laptop). The editor now draws the fill at once and removes floating copper about 0.4 s after you stop changing things (0.05 to 0.09 s per edit on that board). Check and every export always use the finished fill.
- **A thin ground trace through a narrow gap between islands did not join the two pieces of fill** it physically joins: a false "not connected" rubber band, or a connected piece of fill removed as floating. Ground copper now counts as part of the fill.
- **Renaming the ground net moved the fill to another net.** The fill now follows the new name.
- **A net called V- (or V+, VCC, 12V...) could be guessed as ground.** Supply-like names are never guessed; with no GND-like name, the biggest other net is used.
- **Esc while dragging out an island still made the island.** It now cancels it.
- **An island completely hidden under a part body could not be selected** with the Select tool. Click the part, then click it again (not a double-click) to pick the island under it; the Island tool picks islands directly too.
- **The Trace tool could not start on an island**, and clicking an island of the same net did not finish the trace there (it does for pins). Both work now.
- **Auto-place on a board with copper islands** moved parts off their islands (in a test, 8 shorts). It now asks first, and the command line prints a warning.
- The laser "trace adjust would close the gap" warning used the gap as typed, not the real one (never less than the copper clearance).
- Islands are checked whenever they are made or changed, from a file, the editor or a script: sizes are kept between 1 and 400 mm (was 60), an unknown shape becomes a rectangle and a negative corner cut becomes 0.
- If a mouse press never got its release (the window lost focus mid-drag), the fill check now still runs once the mouse stops moving.

## 0.4.0-wip (2026-10-31)

For quick and dirty RF and fast analogue boards, built deadbug / Manhattan style:

- **Copper islands (Island tool, `I`).** Free-standing round or rectangular copper pads. Click to drop one, drag on the board to draw one any size, Shift + click to switch between round and rectangular. With *Select*, drag an island to move it and drag its white corner squares to resize it (the opposite corner stays put); the right panel has exact width, height and corner cut, square/circle size buttons, *Turn 90*, *Duplicate* and *Delete*, and the right-click menu has the same. An island has no net of its own: it joins the net of any pin, trace or jumper pad that touches it, and islands that touch each other share one net. Pins of two different nets on one island are a short, and *Check* says so. A board built from islands needs no traces at all.
- **Ground fill (Board > Ground fill).** The whole board becomes ground copper, with a gap (default 0.6 mm, never less than the copper clearance) cut round every other net. The ground net is guessed (GND, 0V, VSS..., else the net with the most pins) or chosen; it is joined by the fill, so Auto-route leaves it alone and removes its old automatic traces. Other nets' copper can be fattened (default 1 mm, only where it keeps the gap; islands you placed keep their size). Pieces of fill that no ground pin sits on (floating copper) are cut away; a ground pin on a piece of fill that is cut off from the rest gets a yellow rubber band. Turning it on offers to make every pad a deadbug pad (no holes to drill).
- The fill is in every output: the editor, Gerber (with clear polarity, read back by the independent gerbonara library in testing), PDF and SVG prints, and the laser G-code (negative mode then burns only the gaps; isolation mode burns the same as negative on such a board).
- New examples: *Manhattan RF amplifier (copper islands)*, built from islands with no traces, and *RF oscillator (ground fill, routed)*.
- Command line: `solotrace islands board.solotrace out.solotrace [--net GND] [--gap 0.6] [--grow 1] [--deadbug] [--keep-floating] [--off]` turns the ground fill on or off.
- The coloured tool-bar buttons are only as wide as their text, so the bar fits a 1280 px window with the new tool.
- A redraw could be left scheduled when a window closed ("invalid command name ...redraw" in the test output); fixed.

## 0.3.5-wip (2026-10-29)

Rigid-wire refinements, after the author found rigid trunks "still separate, not a true union":

## 0.3.2-wip (2026-10-29)

From the next real board: "the second a rigid line appears the other traces form weird, as if they are repelled":

- **Branches no longer come loose from a trunk.** After routing, and whenever a width changes, wide traces are tidied (straight pad exits, centred ends, bigger chamfers). That tidy could move a rigid trunk away from the branches joined to it, leaving stubs that stop short, overshoot, or a pad cut off (in one rebuild a capacitor pad lost its connection). Now a trace is only reshaped if everything it touched still touches it and every branch end sits at least as deep in it as before; otherwise only its end is moved onto the pad centre, or nothing changes. The corner smoothing obeys the same rule.
- **DRC warns about hanging trace ends** ("dangling"): a trace end that is not joined to a pad, trace or jumper of its net.
- **Board > Keep traces out from under parts** (off by default): Auto-route then goes round part bodies (between DIP rows, under transistors) unless there is no other way. The areas round each pin stay free. On a cramped board this can cost jumpers. Without it, the retries now also count copper under part bodies against a result, so a short route through a chip only wins when going round is much longer.

## 0.3.1-wip (2026-10-29)

Fixes after routing a real board with 0.3.0 gave overlapping tracks and forced jumpers:

- **Overlapping / disappearing tracks fixed.** When a net was only partly routed and then routed again (after a rip-up), the copper it already had was not counted as part of it, so new traces ran straight over the old ones or crossed them. Now every trace already on the board joins its net's group, and new connections end where they meet that copper. This bug was older than 0.3.0; rigid wires made it show up much more often.
- **Retries:** Auto-route now tries up to six net orders (within about 12 seconds) and keeps the best result: fewest unrouted connections, then fewest jumpers, then the shortest copper. The progress bar says "Try n of 6". On seven test boards (the demos, two random boards and a rebuild of the board from the bug report) this took the unrouted connections from 2 to 0 with jumpers allowed.
- **Jumpers are optional:** untick **Jumpers** in the tool bar (or *Board > Auto-route may add jumpers*) and Auto-route never adds wire links; what it cannot route stays as rubber bands. Each net can also refuse jumpers (net panel, or right-click > *Allow jumpers on this net*). Saved with the board.
- **Rigid wires are not stiff:** a rigid (Shift + Wire) pair still gets its own pin-to-pin trace, but the path bends round things like any other trace, and traces now cost a little more under part bodies, so a trunk is less likely to cut through the middle of a chip.
- **Cut tool** (`C`, or right-click a trace > *Cut from here*): click two points on a trace and only the copper between them is removed; the rest of the trace and the net stay. (*Trim*, `X`, still removes a whole section.)

## 0.3.0-wip (2026-10-29)

From the first real design made with SoloTrace (a 555 power oscillator):

- **Rigid wires.** Hold **Shift** while wiring (click or drag from pin to pin) and the pair becomes a rigid link: the router joins those two pins with their own direct trace first, and the net's other pins branch off it. Use it for a power trunk, e.g. a transistor emitter straight to the supply terminal, instead of being chained through a capacitor. Rigid links show as thicker orange rubber bands, the routed trunk is drawn in orange copper, and both can be set or cleared from the right-click menu and the net panel.
- **More visible rubber bands:** thicker yellow lines with a dark edge; the wire being drawn is a dashed line that turns orange while Shift is held.
- **Pin labels.** Common pin layouts per form factor, chosen from a list (right panel or right-click > *Pin labels*): TO-92 E B C / C B E / B C E / D G S / G D S / S G D / small regulators; TO-220 power transistor B C E, MOSFET G D S, IGBT G C E, regulator IN GND OUT, adjustable regulator ADJ OUT IN, negative regulator GND IN OUT, triac, dual diode; TO-247; optocouplers (DIP-4, DIP-6); 555-style timer, single and dual op-amp, dual optocoupler (DIP-8); logic GND/VCC and quad op-amp (DIP-14/16/20); pots; 2- and 3-pin connectors. ## 0.2.1-wip (2026-10-29)

- **Smoother drawing.** Moving the mouse while drawing a wire, a trace, a jumper or placing a part used to redraw the whole board on every move. Now only the moving layer (rubber band, ghost part, hover highlight) is redrawn. On a 40-part test board that is about 6 ms per mouse move instead of about 97 ms; a full redraw went from about 97 ms to about 29 ms (grid points are one item each and only the visible ones are drawn; rubber-band lines are cached until the board changes). Measured in the development sandbox, not on your laptop.

- **Wide traces fixed:** a trace of 1.2 mm or more now leaves each pad with a straight vertical or horizontal run before it turns and ends exactly on the pad centre (before, a widened trace left a TO-92 pad at an angle and ended beside the resistor pad as a blob). Runs after auto-routing and, by default, whenever a width changes; *Tidy* and *Tidy all traces* do it on demand. Chamfers can no longer eat those straight exits.
